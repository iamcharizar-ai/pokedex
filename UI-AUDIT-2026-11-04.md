# UI audit, 4 Nov 2026: closing the visual gap

UI-REVIEW (14 Oct) fixed the look of each screen on its own terms. MOTION-AUDIT gave it timing and UI-AUDIT-2026-10-27 took out the duplicates. Since then two screens have moved a generation ahead: the **card deck** (Team and Lab) and the **Bag**. Everything else was built to the 14 Oct rules, and next to those two it now looks like an earlier app. This audit measures every screen against those two, says where it falls short, and plans how to bring it up. Nothing has been built.

**How it was looked at.** A dev server with `VITE_SUPABASE_DISABLE=1`, and the `empty`, `week`, `late` and `boss` fixtures (plus `&news`, `&band=84`, `&tour=1`, `&clock=` and `&sleep=`). I captured every tab, sub-view, sheet and overlay I could reach at 390 px and 1280 px, with real sprites (the baseline tool masks them), and looked at each picture. Findings are marked **[seen]** when I saw them in a capture, and **[code]** when I read them from the source but could not show them on screen. `npm test` (194 pass, 1 skipped) and `tsc -b` are green. The real ledger was never touched, and nothing in the repo was changed apart from adding this file.

**Two things you should know first.**
1. **The screenshot gate is already red.** `npm run shots -- late boss` reports 8 of 10 changed before any work: Today is 240 px taller, Party is 2,000 px against a baseline of 4,500+, and Shop and Journey differ by about 1%. `tests/heights.txt` still says `boss-party 2006`, but the real page is **24,527 px**. The baselines date from before the card deck, so right now they guard nothing. See question Q1.
2. **The Today scene is not pixel-crisp,** though everything around it assumes it is. The partner's animated GIF (Charmeleon is 69×56, Tangrowth 97×67) is pushed into a 136 or 184 px box with `object-fit: contain`. That is a scale of 1.4× to 2.7×, different for each species and never a whole number. The wild Pokémon's 96 px still is drawn at 80 px, a 0.83× shrink that drops rows of pixels. On top of that, the sun, moon and stars are smooth CSS gradients, and the clouds are pixel paths drawn at 52 to 100 px wide (3.25 to 6.25 px per unit). ## 1. Design DNA of the benchmark

### The card (`MonCard.tsx`, `moncard.css`, `cardkit.tsx`, full-art look)
| Trait | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Anatomy** | A portrait, about 105 × 172 px on a phone. A 2 px metal frame with a 2 px dark edge (`--k-edge #141a33`) and a hard 4 px bottom lift (`0 4px 0 edge`). The art field is a radial gradient of the type colour: light at 46% height, sinking into navy at the edges. A "Lv N" banner lies over the bottom of the art, with a 3 px white sliver for progress through the level. A separate pill under the card holds the hearts. A ribbon across the top edge gives the role ("PARTNER"). |
| **Corner badges** | 24 px circles with the dark edge: the type on its own colour (top left), the signature item on dark glass (top right), a shiny star (top centre). |
| **Sprite** | Enlarged by a whole number from `bbox.json` until the drawn part fills a fixed share of the card. Feet stand at 80% height, with a 3 px hard drop shadow. A scale that would not be whole is drawn from a 4× hard-edged copy, never from a smoothed fraction. |
| **Colour** | The type colour is the only hue on the face. Gold means role or status (partner, fully trained, Elite). Pink means hearts. Green means you can act on it (a berry would take it up a level). Nothing is coloured for decoration. |
| **State** | Carried by the **frame metal**, never by words: silver is normal, gold with a glow is the partner, a shining gold sweep is Elite, an animated rainbow is a Mega. A dashed frame is an empty place, a dimmed and desaturated one is unavailable, a white ring is selected, a dashed outline is being carried, and a gold charge means a hold is filling. |
| **Type** | Lilita One only. Numbers carry a 3 px dark stroke (`paint-order: stroke fill`). **There are no sentences on the card.** The name is hidden in the full-art look. |
| **Icons** | Pixel item sprites in round badges, plus tiny SVG glyphs (heart, up arrow, star) inside the pills. |
| **Density** | Three to a row on a phone with 16/10 px gaps, six on desktop. |
| **Motion** | Each card idles with a 2 px stepped bob on its own beat (a hash of the form). It lifts 3 px on hover and fills with gold during a press. Cards glide to their new places (FLIP), and the heart chain fills link by link. |
| **Show vs say** | Level, progress, bond, type, signature, role, maxed state and whether it can be fed are all drawn. Nothing on the card is written out. |

### The Bag (`baglayout.ts`, Bag in `Play.tsx`, `menu.css` "block-game inventory")
| Trait | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Anatomy** | A grey bevelled panel with pocket tabs on top (an icon each) and a 9-column grid of square slots, each a 2 px inset bevel. |
| **A thing is its icon and a number** | The icon fills 78% of the slot, pixelated. The count is a stroked pixel numeral in the bottom right. Something equipped has a gold marker in the top left. |
| **Labels live one layer down** | The name and what it does are in a hover tooltip or the item's sheet, never on the grid. |
| **State** | The slot's tint: green when picked, gold outline when carried, light blue when hovered over as a drop target. |
| **Agency** | You arrange it yourself, by drag or by Arrange and tap. Things glide only after a move. |
| **Material** | Deliberately its own (grey stone, not the night glass). You said it stays (UI-AUDIT 27 Oct), and it should. |

### What the two share: the measuring stick
1. **Things are objects.** A Pokémon, an item, a reward or a badge is a framed object with depth, whether or not you can press it.
2. **Pixel art at a whole-number scale on a coloured field.** Never shrunk or stretched by a fraction.
3. **State lives in the container** (frame metal, slot tint, ribbon), not in a sentence.
4. **Numbers are short, stroked and placed on the object** (Lv, ×N, ♥ x/y).
5. **Words move one layer down,** into a tooltip, sheet or menu.
6. **Dense grids,** three to nine across.
7. **Hard 2 px dark edges and a hard bottom lift** mean "this is a thing". A soft blur glow is used only for emphasis (partner, drop target).

Every screen below is scored against these seven.

### 2a. The Today scene, taken apart
[seen in 16 captures: four fixtures; dawn, noon, dusk, night; rain and overcast; 390 and 1280]
1. **There is no place.** Two radial-gradient hill blobs, one 13-pixel pine repeated across the width, a flat ground gradient, and two flat green ellipses for pads. Tentacruel (Water/Poison), Skarmory (Steel/Flying) and Porygon (Normal) all stand in the same meadow.
2. **The wild Pokémon is an afterthought.** 80 px on a phone, far up and to the right. Porygon's drawn part is about 30 px. It is the subject of the day and reads like a pebble.
3. **The scaling is broken** (above). It also mixes art styles: the partner is an animated Gen 5-style GIF, the wild Pokémon a static PNG.
4. **Cream HP boxes with black outlines** cover about 45% of the scene on a phone. The partner's box spans the full width and hides its own pad. It has an EXP bar in diagonal stripes, and a legend with swatches ("■ banked ■ today") to explain a two-tone bar. That is old-generation GBA chrome sitting next to the cards.
5. **No lighting.** At 23:30 the partner is as bright as at noon. Time of day only recolours the backdrop.
6. **The sky bodies aren't pixel art.** The sun is a smooth radial gradient with a 26 px blur glow, and the moon a CSS circle with an inset shadow.
7. **Auras are a 4 px inset line** (`index.css:49-50`). A Mega day and a Gigantamax day, the biggest moments in the game, change one border colour, plus a scale and red glow on the sprite for Dynamax.
8. **The bought themes** (beach, cave, night) only swap the palette variables, except the cave, which adds stalactites.
9. **Asleep means grey** (above).

## 5. The Today scene: direction

**Three directions I looked at:**
- **(a) Painted backdrops per habitat** (PNG plates). The best-looking ceiling, but it needs commissioned art for about ten biomes, and time of day can only tint the plate. **No.**
- **(b) Reuse `public/world/`.** It is top-down; the battle is side-on. The perspective is wrong and the licence is unknown. **No.**
- **(c) A procedural pixel diorama at native resolution, scaled by a whole number.** **Yes.**

**What (c) is.** The scene is drawn as a **160 × 100 logical-pixel stage** (phone) or 240 × 110 (desktop), then scaled by the largest whole number that fits (2× or 3× on a phone, 3× or 4× on desktop) with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Every part is a pixel: the sky, sun, clouds, ground, pads and particles. It is drawn with SVG `crispEdges` paths in logical units (as `CLOUD` already is), or a small canvas painter, so it needs **no new art files** and **no sprite URLs change**.

**The layers, back to front:**
1. **Sky:** `paletteAt()` as now, but **banded** into 4 to 6 flat stripes (the pixel-art way to draw a gradient) with a dithered seam. `sky.ts` stays the source of truth; only the drawing changes.
2. **Sun or moon:** pixel sprites (a 9 px disc with a dithered halo; the moon in phases), placed by `bodyAt()`.
3. **Stars and clouds:** single-pixel stars at fixed seeded spots, twinkling in steps. `CLOUD` paths on whole-pixel units, driven by `weatherOf()`.
4. **Far silhouette (biome):** mountains, a sea horizon, ruins, a cave mouth, a city skyline.
5. **Middle (biome):** a tree line, reeds, rocks, ice blocks, power pylons.
6. **Ground:** a perspective strip in three bands, so there is a near and a far.
7. **Pads (biome):** a sand ring, a rock ledge, a lily pad on water, a snow mound, a grass tuft ring. The wild Pokémon's pad is farther away and smaller, the partner's nearer and larger, each with a hard pixel shadow.
8. **Foreground framing:** grass, reeds or rocks overlapping the bottom corners and the edge of the near pad. This is what makes it read as a place and not a backdrop.
9. **Weather:** rain falls between layers 5 and 8 (in front of the hills, behind the foreground), overcast lowers the light, and a clear morning adds two-pixel sun rays.
10. **Light:** one overlay coloured from the palette (warm at dawn, neutral at noon, magenta at dusk, blue at night), set to `multiply` or `soft-light`, **and the same light applied to the sprites** (`filter: brightness() sepia() hue-rotate()` from `darkAt()`). At night the Pokémon are lit by the moon.
11. **Ambient life, one or two loops per biome:** fireflies (meadow at night), embers (volcanic), bubbles (shore), snowfall (ice), drifting leaves (forest), dust motes (cave), sparks (electric). Each is a few pixels in a stepped loop, and none move under reduced motion.

**Habitat follows the wild Pokémon's type** (`SPECIES[form].t`, then `t2`): a pure visual lookup, no rules touched.

A boss changes the biome to an arena. The bought themes become **biome overrides**: beach is the shore pinned to noon, cave the cave biome with no sky, starry night the meadow pinned to 23:00. `THEME_CLOCK` and its test stay as they are.

**The sprites, fixed:**
- The partner: its GIF's natural size × a whole `k` (2× on a phone, 3× on desktop), measured with `lib/fit.ts`.
- The wild Pokémon: its bbox-cropped still × a whole `k` chosen so its drawn height is at least 60% of the partner's. **The wild Pokémon gets real size.**
- Asleep: lit at 75% brightness with a stepped breathing squash (scaleY 0.97) and a pixel "z" bubble. **Never grey.**

**The HUD, redone in the card's language:**
- **Wild nameplate:** a slim dark-glass plate. The type badge (the card's 24 px circle) on the left, the name in Lilita with the 3 px stroke, and **rarity as the plate's metal** (silver common, blue uncommon, pink rare, gold legendary), the same idea as card frames. Below it, the `<Meter kind="hp">` with today's damage as a recent segment and, when a ball is pressed, the ghost (T1). "Gets away in 2 days" becomes a 2-pip timer.
- **Partner plate:** **the card's Lv banner, enlarged**, with an XP sliver that fills and today's part brighter, and "+481" riding its end (T17). The legend goes.
- Both plates are about half the height of today's boxes. On a phone the wild plate sits top-left and the partner plate bottom-right, so the partner's pad is never covered.

**Mega and Gigantamax days, made big:**
- **Mega:** the light shifts prismatic, a key-stone glyph sits on the partner plate, and the plate turns rainbow metal (the card's `.mega`).
- **Gigantamax / Dynamax:** a red cloud vortex fills the sky layer, the ground cracks with red light, and the sprite takes the next whole scale up (`k+1`), not `scale(1.28)`.

**Why this direction.** It keeps everything that works: the clock, the weather from your sleep and `sky.test.mjs` are all untouched, because only how they are drawn changes. It makes pixel art the one material of the scene, which is the benchmark's first rule. It turns each wild Pokémon into an occasion, because the place changes with it and you will see ten biomes in a season. And it needs no art pipeline. The cost is effort: it is the largest item in the plan.

**Tests.** Add `tools/scene.test.mjs` for the pure parts: `biomeOf(t, t2)`, `fitScale(natural, box)` (always a whole number) and `lightAt(minutes, gloom)` (bounded and continuous). `sky.test.mjs` stays as it is.
